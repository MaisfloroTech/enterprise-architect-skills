# EA next steps and plan B

**Step 7 of the case method.** Previous: `6-ea-governance`. Next: `8-ea-glossary`. Use the 90-day commitment from `1-ea-scope-and-objectives`, the contingencies from `3-ea-diagnosis-findings`, the situations in which the CEO wants to be reached from `5-ea-stakeholder-conversations`, and the workstreams and escalation from step 6.

Help an enterprise architect show two things: what happens after the first meeting, and what they will do if it goes wrong. The second one is what most candidates and architects leave out.

## Language

Answer in the language the user writes in (Spanish in, Spanish out). Missing data goes in as UPPERCASE placeholders in the user's language: `ENTER_NAME` / `INGRESA_NOMBRE`.

## Always deliver the complete section

Draft both parts in one answer, then take corrections. After every correction, if the user asks to see it, deliver **the whole section again**.

## Two audiences

- **7.1 Next steps:** executives read it. Neutral wording, no internal tag.
- **7.2 Plan B:** internal review, with the tag from step 3. It is the political preparation for a meeting that fails.

## Flow

### 7.1 Next steps per workstream (days 15 to 90)

| Workstream | Accountable owner | Days 15 to 30 | Days 30 to 60 | Days 60 to 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- Same workstreams and owners as step 6, plus a row for **Enterprise Architecture** (validated map and options by day 30, decision record ready for the committee, approved roadmap with one owner per capability by day 90).
- Every cell is a concrete deliverable that lines up with the 90-day commitment from step 1. No activities without a result ("work on", "follow up").

### 7.2 Plan B: if the first meeting goes wrong

Open with the principle: **a meeting that goes wrong is not fixed in the same room.** Close it calmly, understand what failed in one-to-ones, and come back with an adjusted proposal.

| What can happen | Signal | In the room | In the following 7 days |
|---|---|---|---|

Cover at least these five:
1. The leader with the mandate does not attend or does not take the chair. The last resort is asking whoever gave the mandate (the board) to confirm who leads. Use it only if the leader still does not take it after the one-to-ones, and present it as protecting the initiative, never as pressure.
2. The legitimate priorities clash (speed against stability). Name both as legitimate and take the discussion to data: the map and the traffic light.
3. No decision is taken. Close with at least one agreement (next date, who brings what) and a written summary.
4. Someone rejects their role. Never negotiate it in the group ("let's talk separately"); if it holds, **activate the contingency from step 3** for that risk instead of writing a new one.
5. A sensitive topic comes up in the group. Thank, take it out of the room ("we are reviewing it with the responsible area and will bring it with data"), and hold a one-to-one that same day.

### Relaunch with the CEO

Only if the meeting did not unblock the initiative, a week or so later, after the diagnostic one-to-ones.

| Care | How it is done |
|---|---|

- **It must not look like a rescue:** it comes after the one-to-ones, as a new start, not as a reaction.
- **Never go over the leader's head:** the leader invites the CEO, or at least agrees.
- **Ask the CEO for little:** a 5-minute opening or a written message. Point out that it is one of the situations the CEO agreed to be reached for.

## Rules

- **Never invent facts.** Days come from earlier steps.
- **Deadlines in days.**
- **No abbreviations**, except job titles exactly as the case writes them (CEO, CFO, CTO).
- **What gets adjusted is the form of the proposal, not what the initiative needs.** A plan B that gives everything away to make the next meeting go well is not a plan B.
