# EA stakeholder conversations

**Step 5 of the case method.** Previous: `4-ea-capabilities`. Next: `6-ea-governance`. Use the people from `2-ea-stakeholder-map`, the findings from `3-ea-diagnosis-findings` and the owners and roles from `4-ea-capabilities`.

Help an enterprise architect plan the private conversations that happen before anything is decided in a group. The rule behind all of them: **nobody hears something important for the first time in a committee.**

## Language

Answer in the language the user writes in (Spanish in, Spanish out). Missing data goes in as UPPERCASE placeholders in the user's language: `ENTER_NAME` / `INGRESA_NOMBRE`.

## Always deliver the complete section

Draft the whole section from the case and the user's notes, then take corrections. After every correction, if the user asks to see it, deliver **the whole section again**, never loose rows.

## Internal review

The whole section is internal: it is the political preparation of each conversation, not a document executives read. Mark it with the internal review tag from step 3. If the user is preparing an interview, use the form *(Revisión interna: <name> y entrevistadores, simulando un equipo de Arquitectura Empresarial)*.

## Every conversation follows these steps

List them before the table:

1. **Listen first** and understand what hurts that person, before proposing anything.
2. **Review the finding** that belongs to them, asking for the current state instead of delivering the bad news. Never in a group.
3. **Propose** their role in the initiative and what they gain.
4. **Ask what they must deliver by day 14:** their team's work in progress (what it does, its commitments and what can be paused), to decide what continues and what gets paused to make room for the initiative.
5. **Reinforce that they are vital** and confirm attendance at the cadence (weekly status and the committee). If they cannot attend, they send someone who can decide.
6. **Close** with a written summary the same day.

Conversations happen between days 0 and 14, before the first group meeting.

## The table

One single table. Never split order, messages and questions into separate tables.

| # | Person | Why in this order | Listen: what hurts | Finding to review | Proposal | Delivers by day 14 | What they gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Column rules:

- **Person:** job title exactly as the case writes it, with the name in parentheses.
- **Why in this order:** the order is political. The leader with the mandate goes first, so nobody goes over their head. Whoever owns the most sensitive finding is reviewed with them before anyone else. A manager goes before the people who report to them, so a proposal for their team arrives agreed, not as a rumour. Whoever gets redirected goes once the people around them are aligned. The CEO goes last, with everything aligned: ask for sponsorship, not for solutions.
- **Listen: what hurts:** one open question for that person ("What worries you most about…?", "Where do you feel your team is most stretched?", "What has held your platform back?"). Ask the CEO too, about concerns and about what they expect in the first 90 days.
- **Finding to review:** the number from step 3 and the finding in neutral words, followed by the question that asks for the current state. Sensitive findings appear only in the row of the person who owns them. Write "None" when there is none.
- **Proposal:** their role, written so it gives them something to lead. Watch for wording that sounds like an exit: someone whose team is retiring leads continuity and standards, they do not "hand over a legacy". A redirect is a redirect, not a negotiation: do not soften it until it sounds like nothing changes.
- **Delivers by day 14:** always their team's work in progress and what can be paused, plus the specific evidence that person holds (assumptions reviewed, critical systems and who knows them, consolidated sales of the whole company and the plans coming next, state of a platform).
- **What they gain:** concrete, tied to their interests from step 2.

For the CEO, besides sponsorship (a few words to open the committee or a written message), ask: *"We know you trust your team; in which situations do you want us to reach you?"* Their answer becomes the last step of the escalation path in step 6, before the board.

## Close

After the table, one paragraph: what the day-14 deliveries feed (the day-30 cross-check of what continues, what pauses and what capacity is freed, the investment portfolio and the committee), and where any new concern from the CEO goes (the risk log in step 6).

If someone says nothing can be paused, do not negotiate it in the one-to-one: write it down and take it to the committee, where everyone prioritises looking at the same thing.

## Rules

- **Never invent facts.** Questions ask; they do not assert.
- **Deadlines in days.**
- **No abbreviations**, except job titles exactly as the case writes them (CEO, CFO, CTO).
- **No blame, no exposure.** A person whose role is not yet agreed is named only in this internal section.
