# EA glossary

**Step 8 of the case method.** Previous: `7-ea-next-steps-and-plan-b`. Next: `9-ea-internal-presentation-team` to present the case. Use `ea-unified-case-excel` to put every closed point in one workbook. Use the people from `2-ea-stakeholder-map`, the owners from `4-ea-capabilities` and every term that appears in steps 1 to 7.

Help an enterprise architect close the case with a shared vocabulary: who is who, and what each word means, so nobody has to decode jargon or acronyms in the middle of a meeting.

## Language

Answer in the language the user writes in (Spanish in, Spanish out). Missing data goes in as UPPERCASE placeholders in the user's language: `ENTER_NAME` / `INGRESA_NOMBRE`.

## Always deliver the complete section

Draft both tables in one answer, then take corrections. After every correction, if the user asks to see it, deliver **the whole glossary again**.

## Flow

### 8.1 People

| Job title | Name | Role in the initiative |
|---|---|---|

- Every person or body from the stakeholder map, plus the architecture function.
- Job title as the case writes it. If it is an acronym, write it with its full name: "CEO (Chief Executive Officer)", also inside other cells ("former CEO (Chief Executive Officer) of …").
- Role in one line, taken from what was agreed in steps 4 to 6. If a role is not confirmed yet, say "(to be confirmed)".

### 8.2 Terms

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|

- **Acronyms first**, each written as "ACRONYM (Full Name)" in the term column, with the local equivalent and what that person answers for in the meaning column.
- Then the rest in alphabetical order: method terms (capability, maturity, limiting dimension, anchoring matrix, impact, opportunity, strategic bet, roadmap horizons, team types, smells, trade-off), governance terms (committee, workstream, cadence, traffic light, escalation, weekly progress report, register of risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies, internal review) and case-specific terms (products, platforms).
- One plain sentence per term, no jargon in the definition. When a term is used in English in the case (capability names, team types), say so.

## Rules

- **Never an acronym alone.** Every acronym, in every table and every cell, goes with its full name in parentheses.
- **Only what is used.** A term that does not appear anywhere in steps 1 to 7 stays out, however popular it is. If the user wants to add one for the presentation, add it.
- **Never invent facts** about people or roles.
- **Neutral wording:** the glossary is read by executives, so it carries no sensitive finding.
